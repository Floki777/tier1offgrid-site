Big Berkey vs. Alexapure Pro: Which Filter Actually Wins?

2026-07-29

Water First. Everything Else Is Secondary.

You can survive three weeks without food. Three days without water — and that's in good conditions. In stress, in heat, in a grid-down scenario where you're moving or working hard, you might have 24 hours before dehydration starts impairing your judgment. And impaired judgment kills.

Water storage gets you through short emergencies. Water filtration gets you through everything else.

Two filters dominate the gravity-fed, no-electricity conversation: the Big Berkey and the Alexapure Pro. Both are stainless steel, gravity-fed, no-power filtration systems designed for long-term use. Both have loyal followings. Both have legitimate strengths.

This is a straight-up comparison. No fluff, no paid placement.

The Big Berkey

What It Does Well

The Black Berkey element is one of the most thoroughly tested gravity filter elements on the market. Independent lab testing (not manufacturer-sponsored) has confirmed removal of bacteria, viruses, pharmaceuticals, chloramines, lead, arsenic, fluoride (with PF-2 add-ons), VOCs, and a long list of other contaminants.

The two-element configuration gives you 3.5 gallons per hour — enough for a family of four with reasonable usage. You can add up to four elements to a Big Berkey for even higher throughput.

The system has been in production for decades. Parts are interchangeable. Replacement elements are widely available. The company (New Millennium Concepts Ltd., Texas) has a track record.

Where It Falls Short

Fluoride removal requires add-on PF-2 filters at extra cost. If your municipal water supply is fluoridated and you want it out, budget an extra $50–70 for the PF-2 elements.

The price is higher than the Alexapure. You're paying for the tested elements and the extra flow capacity.

The Alexapure Pro

What It Does Well

The Alexapure Pro costs significantly less than a comparably sized Berkey. For someone on a strict budget who needs gravity filtration and can't afford the Berkey, the Alexapure gets the job done.

Built-in fluoride removal without add-on filters is a legitimate advantage. If you're filtering municipal water and want fluoride out, the Alexapure does it without the additional purchase.

The filter element is rated to 5,000 gallons — close to the Berkey pair. Replacement cost is similar per gallon.

Where It Falls Short

Single-element standard configuration gives you 2.0 gallons per hour — meaningfully slower than a dual-element Berkey. For a family or a group scenario, this matters.

Pathogen removal specs show 99.9% virus reduction versus the Berkey's 99.999%. That's a 100x difference in virus pass-through. For most use cases, 99.9% is more than adequate. But if you're filtering potentially contaminated surface water or water from unknown sources in a grid-down scenario, the Berkey's virus performance is the better number.

Independent third-party testing on the Alexapure elements is less comprehensive than on the Black Berkey. Most data comes from the manufacturer.

The Verdict

If you can afford it: Big Berkey. Better flow rate, better virus numbers, more comprehensive independent testing, and decades of proven field use. Add the PF-2 elements if you want fluoride removal. Total cost: $330–380.

If budget is tight: Alexapure Pro. It filters your water. It removes the major threats. It's better than nothing by several orders of magnitude. $200–240 and you have a functional gravity filter system tonight.

Don't let the comparison stop you from acting. The worst filter you own is infinitely better than the perfect filter you're still saving up for when something goes wrong.

What Else You Need

A filter alone isn't a water plan. Layer it:

  • Storage first — minimum 1 gallon per person per day, 2 weeks stored. 55-gallon food-grade barrels are $50–80. WaterBOB fits in a bathtub (100 gallons, $30). Have this before anything else.
  • Prefilter turbid water — if your source water is cloudy or silty, run it through a coffee filter or bandana first. Sediment clogs filter elements prematurely.
  • Redundancy — LifeStraw or Sawyer Squeeze as backup. $15–30 each. Works when the Berkey is full or you're mobile.
  • Chemical backup — Aquatabs or pool shock (calcium hypochlorite) for disinfection when filtration isn't available. Learn the dosage ratios and keep them written down.

DIY Section: Emergency Gravity Filter

Tier 1 — Prepared Builder: Berkey or Alexapure

Buy one. Today. This is not an optional prep.

A gravity filter is one of the highest-priority purchases in your prep stack. It requires no electricity, no replacement cartridges for years, and filters water from virtually any source — tap, river, lake, questionable well. The Big Berkey's 6,000-gallon lifespan means a family of four using 3 gallons a day gets more than 5 years from one set of elements.

Tier 2 — Emergency Bucket Filter

Two buckets, some materials from a hardware store, and you have a functional gravity filter in 30 minutes.

What You Need

  • 2× food-grade 5-gallon buckets (Home Depot, Lowe's, any restaurant supply — free from restaurants that order pickles or frosting)
  • 1× Berkey Black element OR 1× Sawyer PointONE filter element — the Sawyer threads directly onto a bucket lid
  • A drill and a 5/8" bit (or a knife and a hot nail in true emergency)
  • Short length of 1/2" food-grade tubing

Build It

  1. Drill a hole in the bottom of the upper bucket to accept the filter element
  2. Thread the filter element through from inside the upper bucket, secure the nut on the outside (bottom)
  3. Stack the upper bucket on top of the lower bucket — offset with a notch or spacer so filtered water drains into the lower bucket
  4. Pour source water into the top bucket, let gravity pull it through the filter, collect clean water below

A Sawyer PointONE element filters down to 0.1 microns — removes all bacteria and protozoa, rated to 100,000 gallons. Does not remove viruses. For surface water from rivers and lakes where viral contamination is possible, follow up with chemical disinfection (Aquatabs or bleach).

True Emergency: Boil

If you have nothing else, boil water at a rolling boil for one minute (three minutes above 6,500 feet elevation). Kills bacteria, viruses, and protozoa. Does not remove chemical contamination, heavy metals, or fluoride. But it keeps you alive and that is what matters on Day Zero.

The Mindset

Municipal water exists at the pleasure of the utility, the power grid, and the supply chain that keeps chemical treatment running. Any one of those fails — earthquake, cyberattack, infrastructure failure, prolonged storm — and your tap runs dry or runs dirty.

A quality gravity filter makes you independent of all of it. You can pull water from a creek, a pond, a neighbor's swimming pool, or a rain barrel and drink it safely. That's sovereignty over the most fundamental resource there is.
